worxpad

An open-hardware wireless macropad: 16 low-profile keys, two knobs with push, two round buttons and a 240 × 240 colour display in a CNC aluminium case. Board, case and firmware are generated from code in one repository and checked against one shared file, board.toml.

Design files only. Nothing has been built: no board ordered, no case printed or milled, and the firmware has never run on a chip. Every picture on this page is a render, not a photo.

Where it stands Open the repository

What it is

A desk controller for shortcuts, media and navigation, connected over USB-C or Bluetooth.

  • 16 keys Kailh Choc V2 class switches, 4 × 4 at 19.05 mm pitch, in hot-swap sockets.
  • Two knobs Rotary encoders with push, 24 detents a turn.
  • Two buttons Round caps in front of the display. One steps the layers, one holds the System layer.
  • Display 1.3 inch, 240 × 240 IPS colour panel behind a painted acrylic lens.
  • USB-C Power, charging, data and firmware updates.
  • Bluetooth Up to four hosts, through an nRF52840 in a u-blox BMD-340 module.
  • Battery 1,200 mAh LiPo with a charger, fuel gauge and power switch.
  • Case Two CNC-milled 6061 aluminium shells, bead blasted and anodised black. A PETG print for the prototype.
  • Firmware ZMK, with four layers, ZMK Studio for changing keys, and its own status screen.

worxpad follows the function set of an existing commercial macropad, used as a reference for measurements only. Nothing of its name, artwork, firmware or files is used. Where worxpad differs, and why: docs/reference-device.md.

Design

A wedge, 128.4 by 85.2 mm and 20.6 mm at its highest point, made of two milled shells. The renders come from the case model in Blender. The board in them is the routed board; the parts on it are reference shapes. Materials and colours are proposals.

From the case model

Quick pictures from the case package's own renderer. The lens is drawn opaque, so the display under it does not show. Bought parts are reference shapes, not vendor models. Case notes and checks.

The board

A four-layer board with parts on both faces: 107 parts, 74 nets, 61 of them placed by the board house. It is placed and routed by a generator in the repository and passes KiCad's design-rule check with 0 errors.

The radio module's PCB antenna sits in a bay with no copper or metal near it, behind a plastic insert in the right wall of the case. These pictures show the routed board. Parts are block models with nominal heights, not the makers' 3D models. PCB notes.

Copper layers

Four layers: signals on the top, ground on In1, 3.3 V on In2, signals on the bottom, with short signal hops on both inner layers. Each drawing opens at full size.

Firmware and keymap

ZMK on the nRF52840: USB and Bluetooth LE with four host profiles, four layers, and ZMK Studio over USB and Bluetooth for changing keys without a rebuild.

The display shows the active layer: the knob functions at the top, the 16 key labels in a grid, and a status bar with the layer name, the output and the battery level. The labels are read from the live keymap, so a change made in Studio shows after the next layer change. After 30 seconds without input the backlight goes off and the panel sleeps.

The firmware builds. It has never run on a worxpad, because no board exists yet. There is no clock and no host widget channel yet. Firmware notes.

Specifications

Taken from board.toml and the design files. Nothing here is measured on a real unit.

Radio and MCU
u-blox BMD-340-A-R module (nRF52840). PCB trace antenna in a metal-free bay behind a POM insert in a 30 mm slot in the right wall
Keys
16 Kailh Choc V2 class switches at 19.05 mm pitch, hot-swap sockets. 5 × 4 diode matrix: 16 keys, 2 knob pushes, 2 buttons
Knobs
2 × Bourns PEC12R-4215F-S0024, 24 detents, push
Buttons
2 × Omron B3FS-1000P under round caps
Display
1.3 inch 240 × 240 IPS (ST7789V) on a 24-pin FPC, PWM backlight, painted PMMA lens
Storage
16 MB QSPI NOR flash, reserved for icons; the firmware only puts it to sleep today
Power
1,200 mAh LiPo with protection, TI BQ24072 charger with power path (about 400 mA), TPS7A02 3.3 V LDO, MAX17048 gauge, slide switch on the LDO enable
USB
USB-C receptacle, ESD array, 0.75 A PTC fuse
PCB
4 layers (In1 ground, In2 3.3 V), parts on both faces, built in a routed frame panel with mouse-bite tabs
Case
Two CNC 6061 shells, bead blast and black anodise. 128.4 × 85.2 mm, 20.6 mm at the wedge apex. An FDM PETG variant for a test print
Firmware
ZMK: 4 Bluetooth profiles, USB, ZMK Studio over USB and Bluetooth, 4 layers, own status screen. No clock and no widget channel yet
Updates
Adafruit UF2 bootloader, flashed once over SWD; then worxpad.uf2 by drag and drop
Status
Design files only. Not built

Gates before anyone orders

  1. PCB review. A person must review the signal hops on the inner layers, the ground island under the module and the vias under the module (PCB notes).
  2. VBUS on module pad 66 while 3V3 is off is not read in the nRF52840 Product Specification (D12).
  3. G1 and G2 thread. The check passes (tip 0.45 mm against 0.4) only with a proposed bottoming tap that D6 does not yet record. Needs a decision by the owner (D6).
  4. Knob and RF insert tolerances. The drawings exist; their insert and bore tolerances are proposals that nobody has milled or measured.
  5. USB-C plug test. Plugs with large overmoulds may not seat in the 0.25 mm recess. A plug test gates the case order (D37).
  6. make order exits 0 (it does on 2026-11-14). Run it again after any change; it cross-checks every file the vendors receive.

Electrical, mechanical, firmware and supply points are listed in full under Before you order.
